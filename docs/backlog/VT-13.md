## Why

The doctrine had craft files for two lanes only (DEV 10, QA 9). A grep across all
19 returned **zero** hits for whole classes of decision a senior makes daily:
`isolation level`, `deadlock`, `race condition`, `n+1`, `tracing`, `metric`,
`background job`, `cron`, `rate limit`, `circuit breaker`, `given/when/then`,
`user story`, `vertical slice`, `traceab`, `non-functional`, `out of scope`,
`wip limit`. The BA and PM lanes had **no craft files at all** — they knew the
procedure and nothing about how a senior performs it.

Source: a field study of `nilbuild/developer-roadmap` (@ `74a645b`) — 2,611
curriculum topics read in the authors' own order across 22 roadmaps, with the
load-bearing facts verified against primary sources rather than the roadmap's own
summaries (PostgreSQL isolation docs, the `w3c/wcag` source, MDN,
developer.android.com, docs.swift.org, NIST CSRC, Fowler).

## Out of scope

`design`, `devops`, `sa` and `specialists` still have no competencies. Each is
blocked on the same missing thing: **a lane-step vocabulary for `loads`**. The
step names live in the workflows (`/dev` T0–T6, `/qa` V0–V7, `/ba` B0–B5,
`/pm` P0–P4), and those four roles have none. `competency_check` does not validate
the *value* of `loads`, so an invented step would pass the gate while guaranteeing
the file is never loaded — a green that lies. See Q-15 in the decision queue.
